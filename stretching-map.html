The stretching map from \(\bar\Omega_x^{\circ}\) to \(\Sigma_x\)

This figure accompanies Notes on OpenAI's proof of the symmetric Mahler conjecture, whose notation and license apply. Let \(K\) be a polytope and \(x\in K\). Call \(h\in K^{\circ}\) tight at \(x+iy\), \(y\in \bar\Omega_x\), if \(\langle h,y\rangle=\lambda(\langle h,x\rangle)\), that is, if equality holds in (18). Then the fiber \(\Sigma_x\) is the union of the simplices \(\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\) with \(x\in K_{J,\varepsilon}\) (this is not proved in the note).

The fiber \(\Sigma_x\) can also be seen as the image of a piecewise linear map. The polar of \(\bar\Omega_x\) is \(\bar\Omega_x^{\circ}=\operatorname{conv}\{v/\lambda(\langle v,x\rangle)\colon v\in\{\pm b_l\}\}\). A vertex \(y\) of \(\bar\Omega_x\) corresponds to the facet of \(\bar\Omega_x^{\circ}\) whose vertices are the points \(v/\lambda(\langle v,x\rangle)\) with \(v\) tight at \(x+iy\). For almost every \(x\), by the argument in the proof of Lemma 2, exactly \(d\) vectors \(v\) are tight at each vertex \(y\): for \(x\in K_{J,\varepsilon}\) and \(y=y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) these are \(v=\varepsilon_jb_j\), \(j\in J\). Then the map that is linear on the cone over this facet and sends \(v/\lambda(\langle v,x\rangle)\) to \(v\) stretches the cone onto \(\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\), and these maps agree on common faces of the cones. The figure shows the case \(d=2\).
