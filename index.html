Statement

A convex body is a compact convex set \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^d\) with nonempty interior; it is symmetric if \(K=-K\). Recall that the polar body \(K^\circ\) of a convex body \(K\) containing the origin in its interior is defined by \[K^{\circ}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^d\colon\langle x,y\rangle\le1\ \ \forall\,x\in K\}.\]

Theorem

For every symmetric convex body \(K\subset \mathbb{R}^{d}\),

\[ \operatorname{Vol}(K)\operatorname{Vol}(K^{\circ}) \ge \frac{4^{d}}{d!}.\tag{1} \]

Equality holds if and only if \(K\) is a linear image of a Hanner polytope.

For the definition of Hanner polytopes, see The equality case.

We first assume that \(K\) is a polytope; the general case of the inequality follows by a standard approximation argument, see Lemma 7. Let \(b_1,\dots, b_{m}\) be nonzero, pairwise nonproportional vectors such that \[ K=\{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}\,\colon\,|\langle b_{j},x \rangle| \leq 1\quad\forall\, 1\leq j\leq m \}. \] We will construct, for every \(x \in \operatorname{int} K\), a star-shaped set \(\Sigma_{x}\subset K^{\circ}\), and prove that \[ \int_{K}\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{x})\,d\operatorname{Vol}(x) \geq \frac{4^{d}}{d!}.\tag{2} \] Since \(\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{x})\leq \operatorname{Vol}(K^{\circ})\), this will imply (1).

Complexification

Define \(\lambda:[-1,1]\to[0,\infty)\) by \[ \lambda(t):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{|t|}^{1}\operatorname{arctanh}\left(\sin\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)ds, \] and let \[ D:=\{ w\in\mathbb{C}\,\,\colon\,\,|\mathrm{Re}(w)|\lt 1,\,\,|\mathrm{Im}(w)|\lt\lambda(\mathrm{Re}(w)) \}. \]

Lemma 0
  1. The function \(\lambda\) is continuous, even, positive on \((-1,1)\), vanishes at \(\pm1\), and satisfies \(\lambda''(t)=-\sec\frac{\pi t}{2}\lt0\) on \((-1,1)\).
  2. For every \(0 \lt a\le b\), \[ \lim_{\delta \searrow0}\sup_{a\le c\le b}\Big|\frac{\lambda(1-\delta c)}{\lambda(1-\delta)}-c\Big|=0. \]
  3. The conformal map \(F\) of \(\mathbb{D}\) onto \(D\) with \(F(0)=0\) and \(F'(0)\gt0\) extends to a homeomorphism of \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) onto \(\overline D\), and pushes forward the uniform probability measure on \(\partial\mathbb D\) to the law of \(T+i\varepsilon\lambda(T)\), where \(T\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\) and \(\varepsilon\) is an independent uniform sign.

Figure 1: the domain \(D\)

Since \(\lambda\) is concave and bounded, \(D\) is a bounded convex domain, so the map \(F\) in Lemma 0 exists and is unique by the Riemann mapping theorem, and extends to \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) by Carathéodory’s theorem. Lemma 0 is proved in Appendix: Properties of the function \(F\).

Let \[ \Omega :=\{ z\in \mathbb{C}^{d}\,\,\colon\,\, \langle b_{j},z \rangle \in D \quad \forall\,1\leq j\leq m\}. \] Since \(D\cap \mathbb{R}=(-1,1)\), we have \[ \Omega \cap \mathbb{R}^{d}=\operatorname{int} K. \] For every \(x \in \operatorname{int} K\), denote by \(L_x\) the fiber of \(\overline{\Omega}\) over \(x\): \[ L_{x}:=\{ y\in \mathbb{R}^{d}\,\,\colon\,\,x+iy\in \overline{\Omega} \}, \qquad x \in \operatorname{int} K. \] Since \(\overline{D}=\{ w\colon|\mathrm{Re}(w)|\le1,\,|\mathrm{Im}(w)|\le\lambda(\mathrm{Re}(w)) \}\), \(L_{x}\) is the symmetric polytope \[ L_{x}=\{ y\in \mathbb{R}^{d}\,\,\colon\,\, |\langle b_{j},y \rangle|\leq \lambda\left(\langle b_{j},x \rangle\right)\quad \forall\,1\leq j\leq m \}, \qquad x \in \operatorname{int} K. \] Note that \[ L_x=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\colon\,\langle h,y\rangle\le\lambda(\langle h,x\rangle)\quad\forall\,h\in K^{\circ}\}.\tag{3} \] Indeed, for fixed \(y\in\mathbb{R}^d\), the function \(h\mapsto\lambda(\langle h,x\rangle)-\langle h,y\rangle\) is concave, since \(\lambda\) is. Its minimum over the polytope \(K^{\circ}=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm b_l\}\) is therefore attained at one of the vertices \(\pm b_l\). As \(\lambda\) is even, the inequalities \(\langle\pm b_l,y\rangle\le\lambda(\langle\pm b_l,x\rangle)\) are precisely the inequalities defining \(L_x\), and (3) follows.

A collection of subsets of \(K^{\circ}\)

For \(J\subset[m]\), define a linear map \(B_J:\mathbb{R}^d\to \mathbb{R}^{J}\) by \[ (B_{J}x)_{j}=\langle b_{j},x \rangle ,\qquad j\in J, \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}^d, \] and let \[ \mathcal{J}:=\left\{ J\in{[m]\choose d}\,\,\colon\,\,B_J\text{ is invertible} \right\} \] (note that \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and \(\mathbb{R}^J\) have the same dimension).

Extend \(\lambda\) to all of \(\mathbb{R}\) by setting \(\lambda(t):=0\) for \(|t|>1\). For \(J\in\mathcal{J}\), \(\varepsilon \in \{ -1,1 \}^{J}\) and \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\), let \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) be the unique solution to \[ \langle b_j,y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\rangle=\varepsilon_j\lambda(\langle b_j,x\rangle),\qquad j\in J. \] For \(x\in\operatorname{int} K\), the point \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) satisfies \(d\) of the inequalities defining \(L_x\) with equality, and the vectors \(\{ \varepsilon_jb_j\}_{j\in J}\) are linearly independent. Hence either \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\notin L_x\), or \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) is a vertex of \(L_x\).

Define \[ \Sigma_x:=\bigcup_{y\in \partial L_x}\operatorname{conv}\big(\{0\}\cup\{h\in K^{\circ}\,\colon\,\langle h,y\rangle=\lambda(\langle h,x\rangle)\}\big)\subseteq K^{\circ}.\tag{4} \]

For \(J\in \mathcal{J}\) and \(\varepsilon \in \{ -1,1 \}^{J}\), write \(\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\) for the simplex \[ \Delta_{J,\varepsilon}:=\operatorname{conv} \left( \{0\}\cup \{\varepsilon_{j}b_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right)\subset K^{\circ}, \] and let \[ \mathcal{E}_{J}(x):=\left\{ \varepsilon \in \{ -1,1 \}^{J}\,\, \colon\,\, y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\in L_{x} \right\}. \] Then for every \(x\in\operatorname{int} K\), \[ \Sigma_{x}=\bigcup\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}\bigcup\limits_{\varepsilon \in \mathcal{E}_{J}(x)}\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}.\tag{5} \] We shall only need the inclusion \(\supseteq\), which is immediate: if \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_J(x)\), then \(y=y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) is a vertex of \(L_x\) and \[ \langle\varepsilon_jb_j,y\rangle=\lambda(\langle b_j,x\rangle)=\lambda(\langle\varepsilon_jb_{j},x\rangle)\qquad \text{for all $j\in J$,} \] so \(\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\subseteq\Sigma_x\). The inclusion \(\subseteq\) is proved in Appendix: \(\Sigma_x\) as a union of simplices. In Figure 2 below, each vertex \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) of \(L_x\) with \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_J(x)\) (left) and the simplex \(\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\) (right) are drawn in the same colour.

Lemma 1

For almost every \(x \in \operatorname{int} K\), the simplices \(\{\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\}_{J\in \mathcal{J},\,\varepsilon \in \mathcal{E}_J(x)}\) have disjoint interiors.

Proof. Let \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_J(x)\) and let \(\xi\in\operatorname{int}\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\). Then there exist \(\alpha_{j}>0\) such that \(\sum\limits_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}\lt 1\) and \[ \xi=\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_j\varepsilon_jb_j. \] For \(y\in L_x\), \[ \langle \xi,y\rangle\le\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_j\lambda(\langle b_j,x\rangle), \] with equality if and only if \(y=y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\). So \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) is the unique maximizer of the functional \(\xi\) on \(L_x\), and so it suffices to show that for almost every \(x\), the points \((y_{J,\varepsilon}(x))_{J\in\mathcal{J},\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_J(x)}\) are distinct.

For fixed \(J\), since \(\lambda>0\) on \((-1,1)\), the defining equations of \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) have distinct solutions for different \(\varepsilon\) so the points \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) are distinct. Thus it suffices to consider pairs \((J,\varepsilon), (J',\varepsilon')\) with \(J\neq J'\); fix such a pair and fix \(k\in J'\setminus J\). If \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)=y_{J',\varepsilon'}(x)\), then \[ \langle b_k,y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\rangle=\varepsilon'_k\lambda(\langle b_k,x\rangle).\tag{6} \] Write \(b_k=\sum_{j\in J}c_jb_j\) and use the coordinates \(q_j=\langle b_j,x\rangle\), \(j\in J\), on \(\operatorname{int} K\). Since \(\langle b_j,y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\rangle=\varepsilon_j\lambda(q_j)\), equation (6) reads \[ G(q):=\sum_{j\in J}c_j\varepsilon_j\lambda(q_j)-\varepsilon'_k\lambda\Big(\sum_{j\in J}c_jq_j\Big)=0. \] Since \(b_k\) is not proportional to \(b_j\) for any \(j\neq k\), at least two coefficients \(c_{j_1},c_{j_2}\) are nonzero. Hence, by Lemma 0, \[ \frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial q_{j_1}\,\partial q_{j_2}}=-\varepsilon'_k c_{j_1}c_{j_2}\lambda''\Big(\sum_{j\in J}c_jq_j\Big)\neq0 \] everywhere. By the implicit function theorem, both \(\{G=0,\ \nabla G\neq0\}\) and \(\{\partial G/\partial q_{j_1}=0\}\supseteq\{\nabla G=0\}\) are locally \(C^1\) hypersurfaces, so the level set \(\{G=0\}\) has measure zero. Since there are finitely many pairs \((J,\varepsilon),(J',\varepsilon')\), this proves the lemma. \(\square\)

By (5) and Lemma 1, we get

\[ \operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma_{x})\ge\frac{1}{d!}\sum\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}|\det B_{J}|\cdot|\mathcal{E}_J(x)| \qquad \text{for almost every \(x\in\operatorname{int} K\).} \]

Thus, in order to prove (2), it suffices to prove that \[ \begin{aligned} 4^{d} & \leq \int_{K}\sum\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}|\det B_{J}|\cdot|\mathcal{E}_J(x)|\,d\operatorname{Vol}(x) \\ & = \sum\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon \in \{ -1,1 \}^{J}}|\det B_{J}|\cdot \operatorname{Vol}\left(\{x \in K\,\,\colon\,\,\varepsilon \in \mathcal{E}_{J}(x)\}\right), \end{aligned} \] or equivalently, using the definitions of \(\mathcal{E}_J\), \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\) and \(L_x\), \[ 4^{d}\leq\sum\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon \in \{ -1,1 \}^{J}}|\det B_{J}|\cdot \operatorname{Vol}\left(\{x \in K\,\,\colon\,\,\left| \langle b_{k},y_{J,\varepsilon}(x) \rangle \right| \leq \lambda(\langle b_{k},x \rangle )\quad\forall k\not\in J\}\right).\tag{7} \]

From \(K\) to the cube to the polycircle

Fix \(J\in \mathcal{J}\), \(q\in[-1,1]^J\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^J\), and write \[ z_J(q,\varepsilon):=B_J^{-1}q+iy_{J,\varepsilon}(B_J^{-1}q). \] Since \(\langle b_j,B_J^{-1}q\rangle=q_j\) for \(j\in J\), by the definition of \(y_{J,\varepsilon}\) \[ \langle b_j,z_J(q,\varepsilon)\rangle=q_j+i\varepsilon_j\lambda(q_j)\in\partial D, \qquad j\in J. \] Let \(x=B_J^{-1}q\) and \(y=y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\), so that \(z_J(q,\varepsilon)=x+iy\). Since \(\overline\Omega=\{z\colon\langle b_l,z\rangle\in\overline D\ \forall l\}\) and \(\overline D=\{w\colon|\mathrm{Re}\,w|\le1,\ |\mathrm{Im}\,w|\le\lambda(\mathrm{Re}\,w)\}\), \[ x+iy\in\overline\Omega\iff|\langle b_l,x\rangle|\le1\ \text{ and }\ |\langle b_l,y\rangle|\le\lambda(\langle b_l,x\rangle)\qquad\text{for all }1\le l\le m. \] The conditions \(|\langle b_l,x\rangle|\le1\), \(1\le l\le m\), are equivalent to \(x\in K\); and among the conditions \(|\langle b_l,y\rangle|\le\lambda(\langle b_l,x\rangle)\), those with \(l\in J\) hold with equality by the definition of \(y_{J,\varepsilon}\). Hence \[ z_J(q,\varepsilon)\in\overline\Omega\iff B_J^{-1}q\in K\ \text{ and }\ |\langle b_k,y_{J,\varepsilon}(B_J^{-1}q)\rangle|\le\lambda(\langle b_k,B_J^{-1}q\rangle)\quad\forall k\notin J. \] Since \(q\) and \(\varepsilon\) were arbitrary, and since \[ K\subseteq\{x\colon|\langle b_j,x\rangle|\le1\ \forall j\in J\}=B_J^{-1}([-1,1]^J), \]the change of variables \(q=B_Jx\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} &|\det B_J|\operatorname{Vol}\left(\{x \in K\,\,\colon\,\,\left| \langle b_{k},y_{J,\varepsilon}(x) \rangle \right| \leq \lambda(\langle b_{k},x \rangle )\quad\forall k\not\in J\}\right)\\ &\qquad=\operatorname{Vol}\left(\{q\in[-1,1]^J\,\,\colon\,\,z_{J}(q,\varepsilon)\in \overline\Omega\}\right). \end{aligned} \tag{8} \]

By Lemma 0, \(F\) is a homeomorphism of \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) onto \(\overline D\). Define \(\Phi_J:[-1,1]^J\times\{-1,1\}^J\to(\partial\mathbb D)^J\) by \[ \Phi_J(q,\varepsilon)_j:=F^{-1}\big(q_{j}+i\varepsilon_{j}\lambda(q_{j})\big),\qquad j\in J. \] Extend \(B_J\) (and its inverse) complex-linearly to a map from \(\mathbb{C}^d\) to \(\mathbb{C}^J\); then, by the formula for \(y_{J,\varepsilon}(B_J^{-1}q)\), \[ z_J(q,\varepsilon)=B_J^{-1}\,\mathrm{Re}\,F(\Phi_J(q,\varepsilon))+i\,B_J^{-1}\,\mathrm{Im}\,F(\Phi_J(q,\varepsilon))=B_J^{-1}F(\Phi_J(q,\varepsilon)). \]

Let \(\mu_J\) be the Haar probability measure on \((\partial\mathbb D)^J\). By our choice of \(F\), the function \(\Phi_J\) pushes the product of Lebesgue measure on \([-1,1]^J\) and counting measure on \(\{-1,1\}^J\) forward to \(4^d\mu_J\). Hence, summing (8) over \(\varepsilon\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^J}|\det B_J|\operatorname{Vol}\left(\{x \in K\,\,\colon\,\,\left| \langle b_{k},y_{J,\varepsilon}(x) \rangle \right| \leq \lambda(\langle b_{k},x \rangle )\quad\forall k\not\in J\}\right)\\ &\qquad=4^{d}\mu_J\left(\{\omega\in(\partial\mathbb D)^J\,\,\colon\,\,B_J^{-1}F(\omega)\in \overline\Omega\}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Since \(\overline D=F(\overline{\mathbb D})\) and \(\langle b_j,B_J^{-1}F(\omega)\rangle=F(\omega_j)\in F(\overline{\mathbb D})\) for \(j\in J\), (7) becomes \[ \sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\mu_J\left(\left\{\omega \in (\partial\mathbb D)^J\,\,\colon\,\,\langle b_k,B_J^{-1}F(\omega)\rangle\in F(\overline{\mathbb D})\quad\forall\, k\notin J\right\}\right)\geq 1. \] For \(J\in\mathcal{J}\) let \[ A_J:=F^{-1}\big(B_J(\overline\Omega)\big)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb D}^J, \] with \(F\) applied coordinatewise; then the set \(A_J\) is closed, since \(B_J(\overline\Omega)\) is closed and \(F\) is continuous. For \(\omega\in(\partial\mathbb D)^J\), \(\omega\in A_J\) if and only if \(B_J^{-1}F(\omega)\in\overline\Omega\), so the inequality to prove is \[ \sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\mu_J(A_J)\geq 1. \]

A sequence of submanifolds of the polydisc

Recap:

  • \(b_1,\dots,b_m\) are nonzero vectors spanning \(\mathbb{R}^d\), extended complex-linearly to \(\mathbb{C}^d\).
  • \(\mathcal{J}\) is the set of \(J\in{[m]\choose d}\) for which the linear map \(B_{J}z=(\langle b_{j},z \rangle)_{j\in J}\) from \(\mathbb{C}^{d}\) to \(\mathbb{C}^{J}\) is invertible.
  • \(F:\mathbb D\to\mathbb{C}\) is an injective holomorphic map with a bounded convex image \(D\), which extends to a homeomorphism of \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) onto \(\overline D\) and satisfies \(F(0)=0\),
  • for all \(J\in \mathcal{J}\), we set \(A_J:=F^{-1}(B_J(\overline\Omega))\subseteq\overline{\mathbb D}^J\), where \(\Omega :=\{ z\in \mathbb{C}^{d}\,\,\colon\,\, \langle b_{j},z \rangle \in D \quad \forall\,1\leq j\leq m\}\),

and our goal is to prove that \[ \sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\mu_J(A_J)\geq 1, \] where \(\mu_{J}\) is the Haar probability measure on \((\partial\mathbb D)^{J}\).

Let \(B:=B_{[m]}:\mathbb{C}^d\to\mathbb{C}^m\), so that \(B_J=\pi_J\circ B\) for the coordinate projection \(\pi_J:\mathbb{C}^m\to\mathbb{C}^J\).

For \(k\ge1\) let \(p_k\) be the coordinatewise \(k\)-th power, \(p_k(v)=(v_j^k)_j\), on \(\mathbb{C}^m\) and on each \(\mathbb{C}^J\); then \(\pi_J\circ p_k=p_k\circ\pi_J\). Let \[ dV_J:=\bigwedge_{j\in J}\frac{i}{2}\,dv_j\wedge d\bar v_j \] denote the volume form of \(\mathbb{C}^J\), let \(dV:=dV_{[d]}\) denote the volume form of \(\mathbb{C}^d\), and let \(M_{k}\) be the \(d\)-dimensional complex submanifold \[ M_k:=p_k^{-1}(\mathbb B_m)\cap F^{-1}\big(B(\mathbb{C}^d)\big)\subseteq\mathbb D^m, \] where \(\mathbb B_m\) denotes the open Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb{C}^m\).

The map \[ \phi:=F^{-1}\circ B \] is a biholomorphism of \(\Omega\) onto \(F^{-1}(B(\mathbb{C}^d))\), so \(M_k=\phi(\Omega_k)\) where \[ \Omega_k:=\{z\in\Omega\colon p_k(\phi(z))\in\mathbb B_m\}. \] For \(u\in M_k\) and \(J\in\mathcal{J}\), \[ F(u)=B\,B_J^{-1}F(\pi_Ju), \] so \(\pi_J\) is injective on \(M_k\) and \(\pi_J(M_k)\subseteq A_J\).

Define a \(2d\)-form \(\sigma_{k}\) on \(M_{k}\) by \[ \sigma_k:=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}(\pi_J|_{M_k})^*\,p_k^*\,dV_J. \]

Lemma 2

Each term of \(\sigma_k\) is a nonnegative \(2d\)-form on \(M_k\), and the integral \[ \int_{M_k}\sigma_k \] is the \(2d\)-dimensional volume of \(p_k(M_k)\), counted with multiplicity.

Proof. Let \(f_{k}:=p_k\circ\phi\), let \(P:=Df_k(z)\), and for \(J \in {[m]\choose d}\) denote by \(P_J\) the corresponding minor of \(P\). Since \(f_k=(p_k\circ F^{-1})\circ B\) with \(p_k\circ F^{-1}\) acting coordinatewise, \(P_J\) is \(B_J\) multiplied on the left by a diagonal matrix, so \(\det P_J=0\) for \(J\notin\mathcal{J}\). Then \[ \phi^*\sigma_k=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}(\pi_J\circ f_k)^*dV_J=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}|\det P_J|^2\,dV=\sum_{J\in{[m]\choose d}}|\det P_J|^2\,dV, \] where the first equality follows from \(\pi_J\circ p_k=p_k\circ\pi_J\), the second holds because a complex-linear map \(T\colon\mathbb{C}^d\to\mathbb{C}^J\) has real Jacobian \(|\det T|^2\), and the third holds since \(\det P_J=0\) for all \(J\notin\mathcal{J}\). By Cauchy–Binet, \(\sum_{J\in{[m]\choose d}}|\det P_J|^2\) is the \(2d\)-dimensional Jacobian of \(f_k\) at \(z\), and the lemma follows. \(\square\)

Lemma 3

For all \(J \in \mathcal{J}\),

\[ \limsup_{k \to \infty} \frac{1}{k^{d}}\int_{M_{k}}(\pi_J|_{M_k})^*p_k^*dV_J\leq \frac{\pi^{d}}{d!}\mu_{J}(A_{J}). \]

Proof. Write \[ R_{J}:=\{\rho\in\mathbb{R}^J\,\,\colon\,\,\sum_j\rho_j^2\lt 1,\,\,\rho_{j}>0\,\,\forall j\in J\} \] and \(\rho^{1/k}\omega:=(\rho_j^{1/k}\omega_j)_{j\in J}\). Then \[ \begin{align*} \limsup_k\frac{1}{k^d}\int_{M_k}(\pi_J|_{M_k})^*p_k^*dV_J &\le\limsup_k\frac{1}{k^d}\int_{A_J\cap p_k^{-1}(\mathbb B_J)}p_k^*dV_J\\ &=\limsup_k\,(2\pi)^d\int_{R_{J}\times(\partial\mathbb D)^J}\mathbf 1_{A_J}(\rho^{1/k}\omega)\prod_j\rho_j\,d\rho\,d\mu_J(\omega)\\ &\le(2\pi)^d\int_{R_{J}\times(\partial\mathbb D)^J}\mathbf 1_{A_J}(\omega)\prod_j\rho_j\,d\rho\,d\mu_J(\omega)\\ &=\frac{\pi^d}{d!}\,\mu_J(A_J). \end{align*} \] The first inequality holds because \(\pi_J\) is injective on \(M_k\) and \(\pi_J(M_k)\subseteq A_J\cap p_k^{-1}(\mathbb B_J)\). In the second passage we applied the substitution \(v_j=\rho_j^{1/k}\omega_j\) and used \(p_k^*dV_J=k^d(2\pi)^d\prod_j\rho_j\,d\rho\otimes\mu_J\). The second inequality is Fatou's lemma, since \(A_J\) is closed and \(\rho^{1/k}\omega\to\omega\), so \(\limsup_k\mathbf 1_{A_J}(\rho^{1/k}\omega)\le\mathbf 1_{A_J}(\omega)\). \(\square\)

A lower bound on the volume of the image of a holomorphic function

Summing Lemma 3 over \(J\in \mathcal{J}\) we get \[ \limsup_{k \to \infty} \frac{1}{(\pi k)^{d}}\int_{M_{k}}\sigma_{k}\leq \frac{1}{d!}\sum\limits_{J\in \mathcal{J}}\mu_{J}(A_{J}). \] The proof will thus be finished once we prove that \[ \frac{1}{(\pi k)^{d}}\int_{M_{k}}\sigma_{k}\geq \frac{1}{d!}. \]

Recall that \[ M_k:=p_k^{-1}(\mathbb B_m)\cap F^{-1}\big(B(\mathbb{C}^d)\big), \] and that, by Lemma 2, the integral \(\int_{M_k}\sigma_{k}\) is the \(2d\)-dimensional volume of the image \(p_{k}(M_{k})\), counted with multiplicity. Thus it suffices to apply the following key lemma:

Lemma 4

Let \(d\geq 1\) and \(m\geq d\). Let \(U\subset \mathbb{C}^{d}\) be an open set containing the origin, and let \(f:U\to \mathbb{C}^{m}\) be a holomorphic function satisfying

  1. \(f^{-1}(0)=\{ 0 \}\)
  2. \(|f(z)|\le C|z|^{k}\) on a neighbourhood of \(0\), for some integer \(k\geq 1\) and some \(C>0\),
  3. for every \(s \lt 1\) the sublevel set \(U_{s}:=\{ |f|\lt s \}\) is relatively compact in \(U\).

Then the \(2d\)-dimensional volume of \(f(U_{1})\), counted with multiplicity, is at least \(\frac{(\pi k)^{d}}{d!}\).

Let us verify that the hypotheses of Lemma 4 are satisfied with \(f=f_k=p_{k}\circ F^{-1}\circ B\). The set \(\Omega\) is open and contains \(0\), and \(f_k\) is holomorphic on \(\Omega\) because \(B(\Omega)\subseteq D^m\) and \(F^{-1}\) acts coordinatewise. In the notation of the lemma, we have \(U_1=\{ |f_{k}|\lt 1 \}=\Omega_k\), so \(f_k(U_1)=p_k(\phi(\Omega_k))=p_k(M_k)\). Since \(F(0)=0\) and \(F\) is injective, \(f_k(z)=0\) if and only if \(Bz=0\), that is, \(z=0\), because \(b_1,\dots,b_m\) span \(\mathbb{R}^d\).

Since \(F^{-1}\) is holomorphic near \(0\) and \(F^{-1}(0)=0\), there are \(\delta>0\) and \(C>0\) with \(|F^{-1}(w)|\le C|w|\) for \(|w|\lt\delta\). Since \(|f_k(z)|^{2}=\sum_{j}\big|F^{-1}(\langle b_j,z\rangle)\big|^{2k}\), for \(z\) near \(0\) we have \[ |f_k(z)|\le\sqrt m\,C^{k}\max_j|\langle b_j,z\rangle|^{k}. \] Since \(B\) is injective, \(\max_j|\langle b_j,\cdot\rangle|\) is a norm on \(\mathbb{C}^d\), so \(|f_k(z)|\le C'|z|^{k}\) for some \(C'>0\) near the origin.

Theorem 8 (Lima,6 Hansen–Lima7)

Let \(\|\cdot\|\) be a norm on \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\). Suppose that every triple of points has a median in \((\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|)\). Then the unit ball of \(\|\cdot\|\) is a linear image of a Hanner polytope.

Proof. A normed space has the 3.2 intersection property if any three pairwise intersecting closed balls, of arbitrary radii, have a common point.8 Let the closed balls of radii \(\rho_i\) centered at \(x_i\), \(i=1,2,3\), intersect pairwise. Let \(m\) be a median of the centers and \(r_i=\|m-x_i\|\), so \(r_i+r_j=\|x_i-x_j\|\le\rho_i+\rho_j\). If every \(r_i\le\rho_i\), then \(m\) is a common point. Otherwise say \(t:=r_1-\rho_1>0\), so \(r_j+t\le\rho_j\) for \(j=2,3\). The point \(z=m+\frac t{r_1}(x_1-m)\) has \(\|z-x_1\|=\rho_1\) and \(\|z-x_j\|\le t+r_j\le\rho_j\). Hence the space has the 3.2 intersection property, and the conclusion is Corollary 7.4 of Hansen–Lima.9 \(\square\)

Suppose \(\operatorname{Vol}(K)\operatorname{Vol}(K^{\circ})=4^{d}/d!\). By Lemma 7 and Theorem 8, the unit ball \(K^{\circ}\) of \(\|\cdot\|_{K^{\circ}}\) is a linear image of a Hanner polytope. Polarity swaps \(\oplus_1\) and \(\oplus_\infty\) and commutes with linear maps up to transpose-inverse, so \(K=(K^{\circ})^{\circ}\) is one too.

Conversely, for symmetric bodies \(A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\), \(A'\subset\mathbb{R}^{l}\), the fiber of \(A\oplus_1A'\) over \(x\in A\) is \((1-\|x\|_A)A'\), so \[ \operatorname{Vol}(A\oplus_1A')=k\operatorname{Vol}(A)\operatorname{Vol}(A')\int_0^{1}t^{k-1}(1-t)^{l}\,dt=\frac{k!\,l!}{(k+l)!}\operatorname{Vol}(A)\operatorname{Vol}(A') \] and \[ \operatorname{Vol}(A\oplus_\infty A')=\operatorname{Vol}(A)\operatorname{Vol}(A'). \] Since polarity swaps the two sums, both satisfy \(\operatorname{Vol}(A\oplus A')\operatorname{Vol}\big((A\oplus A')^{\circ}\big)=\operatorname{Vol}(A)\operatorname{Vol}(A^{\circ})\cdot\operatorname{Vol}(A')\operatorname{Vol}(A'^{\circ})\big/\binom{k+l}{k}\). A segment gives \(4\), so induction gives \(4^{d}/d!\) for every Hanner polytope, and linear maps do not change the volume product. \(\square\)

Define an analytic function \(F\) on the unit disc \(\mathbb{D}\subset \mathbb{C}\) by \[ F(z):=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)^{2}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}. \]

Proof of Lemma 0. The integrand \(\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sin\frac{\pi s}{2}\big)\) is positive on \((0,1)\) and \(O\big(\log\frac{1}{1-s}\big)\) as \(s\uparrow1\), so \(\lambda\) is continuous on \([-1,1]\), \(\lambda(\pm1)=0\), and \(\lambda>0\) on \((-1,1)\). For \(0\lt |t| \lt 1\), since \(\lambda\) is even, \[ \lambda'(t)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{arctanh}\Big(\sin\frac{\pi t}{2}\Big), \] and this also holds at \(t=0\), because the right-hand side is continuous and vanishes there. Differentiating again, \[ \lambda''(t)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\frac{\pi}{2}\cos\frac{\pi t}{2}}{1-\sin^{2}\frac{\pi t}{2}}=-\sec\frac{\pi t}{2}\lt0,\qquad -1\lt t \lt 1, \] so \(\lambda\) is strictly concave on \([-1,1]\); being also even, it attains its maximum at \(0\). It follows that \(D\) is a bounded convex domain.

The series converges absolutely on \(\overline{\mathbb D}\), so \(F\) is continuous there, \(F(0)=0\), \(F'(0)=8/\pi^{2}\gt0\), \(F(\pm1)=\pm\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\sum_j(2j+1)^{-2}=\pm1\), and \(F(\bar z)=\overline{F(z)}\). Differentiating the series and multiplying by \(z\), we get \[ zF'(z)=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\operatorname{arctanh}z, \] which extends analytically across \(\partial\mathbb D\setminus\{\pm1\}\). For \(0 \lt \theta\lt\pi\), \(\operatorname{arctanh}e^{i\theta}=\frac12\log\big(i\cot\frac\theta2\big)\), so \[ \frac{d}{d\theta}F(e^{i\theta})=\frac{8i}{\pi^{2}}\operatorname{arctanh}e^{i\theta}=-\frac{2}{\pi}+\frac{4i}{\pi^{2}}\log\cot\frac{\theta}{2}. \] Hence \(\mathrm{Re}\,F(e^{i\theta})=1-2\theta/\pi=:t\), and, by \(\log\cot\frac{x}{2}=\operatorname{arctanh}(\cos x)\), \(\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Im}\,F=-\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sin\frac{\pi t}{2}\big)=\lambda'(t)\) with \(\mathrm{Im}\,F=0\) at \(t=\pm1\).

So \(\mathrm{Im}\,F(e^{i\theta})=\lambda(t)\), and since \(F(\bar z)=\overline{F(z)}\), \[ F(e^{\pm i\theta})=t\pm i\lambda(t),\qquad t=1-\frac{2\theta}{\pi},\quad 0\le\theta\le\pi. \] Thus \(F\) maps the upper and the lower semicircle bijectively onto the upper and the lower half of \(\partial D\), and \(F|_{\partial\mathbb D}\) is a homeomorphism onto the Jordan curve \(\partial D\), which it traverses counterclockwise.

Let \(w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial D\). As \(r\uparrow1\), the curves \(\theta\mapsto F(re^{i\theta})\) converge uniformly to \(\theta\mapsto F(e^{i\theta})\), so for \(r\) close to \(1\) their winding number around \(w\) is \(1\) if \(w\in D\) and \(0\) if \(w\notin\overline D\). By the argument principle, \(F\) takes every value in \(D\) exactly once in \(\mathbb D\) and no value outside \(\overline D\). Since \(F(\mathbb D)\) is open, it does not meet \(\partial D\). Hence \(F\) is a biholomorphism of \(\mathbb D\) onto \(D\), and, with the boundary correspondence, a continuous bijection of the compact set \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) onto \(\overline D\), hence a homeomorphism.

Let \(\zeta\) be uniform on \(\partial\mathbb D\), and write \(\zeta=e^{i\varepsilon\theta}\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(\theta\in(0,\pi)\), which is possible almost surely. Then \(\varepsilon\) is a uniform sign, and \(\theta\) is uniform on \((0,\pi)\) and independent of \(\varepsilon\). So \(T:=1-2\theta/\pi\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\) and independent of \(\varepsilon\), and \(F(\zeta)=T+i\varepsilon\lambda(T)\).

It remains to prove the endpoint asymptotics. Substituting \(s=1-\tau\) in the definition of \(\lambda\) and using \(\operatorname{arctanh}(\cos u)=\log\cot\frac u2\), \[ \lambda(1-\eta)=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\eta}\log\cot\frac{\pi\tau}{4}\,d\tau=\frac2\pi\,\eta\Big(\log\frac1\eta+1+\log\frac4\pi\Big)+O(\eta^{3}). \] The ratio at \(\eta=\delta c\) and \(\eta=\delta\) is \(c\,\big(1+O(1/\log\tfrac1\delta)\big)\), uniformly for \(c\in[a,b]\). \(\square\)

Appendix: \(\Sigma_x\) as a union of simplices

We prove the inclusion \(\subseteq\) in the equality \(\Sigma_{x}=\bigcup_{J\in \mathcal{J}}\bigcup_{\varepsilon \in \mathcal{E}_{J}(x)}\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\) stated after (4); it is not used elsewhere in this note. Call \(h\in K^{\circ}\) active at \(y\in L_x\) if \(\langle h,y\rangle=\lambda(\langle h,x\rangle)\), so that (4) reads \[ \Sigma_x=\bigcup_{y\in \partial L_x}\operatorname{conv}\big(\{0\}\cup\{h\in K^{\circ}\,\colon\,h\text{ is active at }y\}\big). \]

Fix \(y\in\partial L_x\) and let \[\xi\in\operatorname{conv}\big(\{0\}\cup\{h\in K^{\circ}\colon h\text{ is active at }y\}\big).\]

Let \(h\in K^{\circ}\) be active at \(y\). Since \(K^{\circ}=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm b_l\}\), \(h\) is a convex combination, with positive weights, of some of the vectors \(\pm b_l\). The function \(\lambda(\langle \cdot,x\rangle)-\langle \cdot,y\rangle\) is concave, nonnegative on \(K^{\circ}\) by (3), and vanishes at \(h\), so it vanishes at each of these vectors, that is, they are active at \(y\). Consequently, \[ \xi=\sum_v\beta_v\,v,\qquad\beta_v\ge0,\quad\sum_v\beta_v\le1, \] where \(v\) ranges over the vectors \(\pm b_l\) active at \(y\). Among all such representations of \(\xi\), choose one with the fewest nonzero coefficients. Then the vectors \(v\) with \(\beta_v>0\) are linearly independent.

The points of \(L_x\) at which every \(v\) with \(\beta_v>0\) is active form a face of \(L_x\) containing \(y\); let \(y'\) be a vertex of this face. Then \(y'\) is a vertex of \(L_x\), so the vectors active at \(y'\) span \(\mathbb{R}^d\). Moreover, \(b_l\) and \(-b_l\) are never both active at \(y'\), since this would give \(\lambda(\langle b_l,x\rangle)=-\lambda(\langle b_l,x\rangle)\), whereas \(\lambda(\langle b_l,x\rangle)>0\) because \(|\langle b_l,x\rangle|\lt 1\). Hence the linearly independent vectors \(\{v\colon\beta_v>0\}\) can be completed, by vectors active at \(y'\), to a basis of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) of the form \(\{\varepsilon_jb_j\}_{j\in J}\) with \(J\in\mathcal{J}\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^J\). Since every \(\varepsilon_jb_j\) is active at \(y'\) and \(\lambda\) is even, \[ \langle b_j,y'\rangle=\varepsilon_j\lambda(\langle b_j,x\rangle),\qquad j\in J, \] that is, \(y'=y_{J,\varepsilon}(x)\). As \(y'\in L_x\), we get \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_J(x)\), and \(\xi=\sum_v\beta_vv\in\Delta_{J,\varepsilon}\). \(\square\)
